Macroeconomic analysis of Thailand begins with a disciplined framework. GDP scale shows the size of the economy and the potential tax base; real GDP growth shows momentum and cyclical pressure; GDP per capita places the development challenge at the level of household income. Price stability determines whether growth can be converted into real purchasing power and credible public budgeting. In Thailand, inflation, exchange-rate movement, and reserve cover should be read as a single stability system: inflation affects real wages and procurement costs; exchange-rate depreciation changes import prices, external debt service, and remittance values; reserves indicate how much external pressure can be absorbed before adjustment becomes abrupt.
