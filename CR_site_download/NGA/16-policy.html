16.1 Evolution of National Development Strategy

Nigeria's development strategies have moved through changing diagnoses of the same structural problem. Earlier national plans emphasized state-led development; structural adjustment shifted toward liberalization; Vision 20:2020 aimed at global economic ranking; NEEDS and SEEDS connected reform to federal and state planning; the Economic Recovery and Growth Plan responded to recession and oil shock; and the Economic Sustainability Plan addressed the COVID-19 crisis. Each plan left institutional lessons about financing, implementation, and political ownership.

16.2 National Development Plan, Agenda 2050, and Renewed Hope

The National Development Plan 2021-2025 and Nigeria Agenda 2050 attempt to connect medium-term delivery to long-term transformation. They emphasize infrastructure, macroeconomic stability, industrialization, human capital, social inclusion, security, governance, and digital transformation (Federal Ministry of Finance, Budget and National Planning, 2021; Federal Ministry of Budget and Economic Planning, 2023). The Renewed Hope agenda under President Bola Tinubu has placed subsidy removal, exchange-rate reform, tax reform, investment, food security, infrastructure, security, and social protection at the center of the current policy cycle.

16.3 Macroeconomic Reform: Subsidy, Exchange Rate, Tax, and Debt

The recent macro reform package is historically significant because it tries to correct long-standing distortions in fuel pricing, foreign exchange, and deficit financing. The administrative challenge is sequencing: stabilization can restore confidence, but its political sustainability depends on credible cash transfers, food supply response, job creation, and visible improvements in public spending.

16.4 Industrialization, Agriculture, Energy Transition, and Digital Economy

Industrial and sectoral policy now turns on agriculture, gas, power, refinery capacity, solid minerals, manufacturing, digital services, ports, and regional trade. Nigeria's digital-economy strategy is particularly important because private innovation already exists; the public task is to provide infrastructure, regulation, identity, cybersecurity, and skills.

16.5 Public Administration Reform and Human-Capital Strategy

Public administration reform is the hinge between strategy and outcome. FCSSIP25, payroll controls, performance management, digital records, procurement reform, and PFM modernization should be treated as development policies, not internal housekeeping. Without administrative capability, national plans become aspirational documents rather than instruments of transformation.
