13. Education, Health, and Welfare

Enrollment trends show the human-capital base of industrial upgrading. The binding constraint increasingly lies in quality, technical skills, higher education relevance, and adult reskilling.
Life expectancy summarizes the cumulative effect of nutrition, disease control, primary care, living standards, and public-health administration. For Vietnam, the long-run gain also signals the coming policy burden of aging and chronic disease.
Infant mortality captures the reach and quality of maternal, neonatal, sanitation, nutrition, and primary-care systems. Falling mortality indicates broad social progress, while the remaining level points to territorial and service-quality gaps.
Health expenditure as a share of GDP indicates the fiscal weight of the health system. Sustainability now depends on financing that can keep pace with ageing and rising expectations for quality care.
Hospital beds measure part of physical service capacity, but not quality or territorial distribution. The indicator is most useful when read with financing, workforce, referral systems, and regional access.
