ASEAN comparative lens (2026). Cambodia should be read in ASEAN comparison as a post-conflict, party-dominant, small open economy linked to garments, tourism, construction, and Chinese finance. Cambodia's fiscal-risk profile begins with a linked question: is the state collecting enough recurring revenue to finance its expenditure commitments without moving onto a debt path that raises interest costs? General-government revenue is 14.6% of GDP and expenditure is 17.3% of GDP in the IMF series; tax revenue is 12.2% of GDP in the World Bank series; gross debt is 27.

Fiscal analysis is one of the clearest ways to test the Cambodian state. Revenue collection has improved, and the Ministry of Economy and Finance has become a core reform institution through budget modernization, treasury control, FMIS, procurement reform, and public investment management. Yet fiscal capacity remains constrained by a narrow tax base, informality, customs and VAT dependence, local revenue weakness, contingent risks, and demands for education, health, infrastructure, and social protection. The fiscal question is not only whether debt is low; it is whether recurring revenue, budget credibility, procurement integrity, and audit follow-up can support Cambodia's development ambitions.

Fiscal indicators are state-capacity indicators. Cambodia's low debt ratio is a strength, but fiscal space depends on revenue mobilization, customs and tax administration, public investment quality, expenditure efficiency, and contingent risks from real estate, credit, and infrastructure projects. IMF surveillance and World Bank updates are especially useful because they connect fiscal buffers to external shocks, property-sector stress, financial vulnerabilities, and the need for targeted reform.

The fiscal interpretation combines IMF general-government diagnostics with World Bank fiscal indicators because the two sources illuminate different parts of Cambodia's revenue, expenditure, debt, and risk profile (International Monetary Fund, 2025; World Bank, 2025b).
