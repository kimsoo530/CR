ASEAN comparative lens (2026). Thailand should be read in ASEAN comparison as an upper-middle-income mainland hub shaped by tourism, automotive manufacturing, civil-military politics, and rapid aging. It differs from Vietnam's newer FDI-led industrial acceleration, Indonesia's scale-driven domestic market, Singapore's high-capacity city-state, and Cambodia-Laos resource/corridor models because its central challenge is upgrading a mature but politically constrained middle-income economy. For this section, the regional comparison should compare the administrative machinery with ASEAN peers by asking whether coordination relies on central ministries, local governments, party structures, federal bargains, or compact city-state capacity. Financial administration in Thailand links political promises to authorized spending, contracts, payments, accounting, and audit. The latest comparable IMF value for the general-government balance is -1.9 percent of GDP in 2025, indicating a moderate general-government deficit. The approximate five-year movement is 2.6 percentage points, so the fiscal trend must be read together with revenue capacity, expenditure pressure, interest burden, and the credibility of budget execution.
