## 16.5 Public Administration Reform and Human-Capital Strategy

### 16.6 Strategic Assessment

Myanmar’s verified strategy archive supports a structured but qualified assessment. Before 2021, MSDP supplied the strongest national planning architecture, with a long horizon, five goals, three pillars, action plans and an implementation matrix. After 2021, the official MERP announcement shows an effort to frame economic recovery through action plans, action lines, lead agencies and KPIs, but the full document and implementation record remain unavailable locally. The FDC supplies an alternative federal-democratic and interim constitutional framework that connects economic and social policy to federal power-sharing, rights and institutional reconstruction.

The central analytical conclusion is not that one plan succeeded or failed. It is that Myanmar moved from a formally unified national planning architecture toward a contested strategic environment in which different actors articulated different institutional and developmental pathways, while the evidence needed to evaluate implementation became more fragmented. The appropriate publication standard is consequently to document each framework’s stated design, date and issuer; separate official claims from observed operation; and mark performance, territorial reach and inter-framework succession as unresolved where the evidence does not close them.
