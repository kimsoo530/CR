## 11.2 Transport, Logistics, Ports, and Corridors

Transport infrastructure determines whether public personnel, goods, and services can move through Myanmar's administrative chain. Roads, rivers, ports, border crossings, and logistics corridors connect producers to markets and central institutions to peripheral areas. Their value depends on maintenance, security, seasonal access, and administrative coordination, not only on construction.

The current archive contains logistics and trade indicators but no complete corridor-level dataset. Aggregate trade or infrastructure measures cannot establish that a route is open, safe, or equally accessible across the country, particularly where conflict and displacement alter routes.

Transport should therefore be read with fiscal maintenance, external trade, humanitarian access, and local service delivery. Current corridor performance remains unresolved.
