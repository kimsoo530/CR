## 7.3 Public Financial Management and Budget Reform

### 7.3 Intergovernmental Finance and Subnational Fiscal Structure

The Constitution gives Region/State governments formal budget, fund and revenue responsibilities, while the Financial Commission provides the constitutional channel for Union Fund distributions, grants and loans. This is a system of intergovernmental finance in legal design, but not necessarily a system of strong fiscal autonomy. Region/State expenditure is linked to the Union budget, and the Constitution requires coordination with the Union Government for Region/State civil-service organization and appointments.

The fiscal question is therefore not simply whether Regions and States have their own funds. It is whether they can plan, receive, spend and account for resources with sufficient predictability to carry out their assigned functions. The 2019 Asia Foundation analysis, used for the Chapter 5 baseline, describes ambiguities in accountability and expenditure responsibilities below the Union level. That evidence is useful for the pre-2021 institutional setting but does not establish current transfer performance.

The statistical appendix contains no subnational revenue or expenditure series. No national official subnational budget tables were found in the existing archive, and no new collection was undertaken in this focused batch. The formal Union Fund and Region/State fund structure can therefore be explained without presenting a numerical decentralization ratio or claiming uniform fiscal reach.

This distinction matters under conflict conditions. A formally assigned Region/State function may be funded through a Union-linked process, implemented through a departmental chain, interrupted by conflict, or performed by another local authority. The fiscal architecture identifies responsibility; it does not prove that funds, personnel and services reached the intended locality.
