## 12.3 Agriculture, Industry, Services, and Productivity

Employment structure determines whether Myanmar's sectoral output becomes broad-based income. Agriculture is closely connected to rural livelihoods and local infrastructure; industry depends on energy, logistics, finance, and regulation; services include both high-productivity activities and large informal segments. ILO and World Bank indicators provide different views of these sectors and should not be merged without definition checks (International Labour Organization, 2025; World Bank, 2025).

Productivity is therefore an institutional question as well as an economic one. Land administration, transport, electricity, skills, credit, and market access influence what workers and firms can produce. Conflict and displacement may change employment without a corresponding national-account reclassification.

The evidence supports linking labor and macroeconomic structure. Current productivity by region and sector remains insufficiently documented.
