## 4.5 Local Politics and Real Power Relations

Local politics connects national institutions to Regions, States, districts, townships, wards, village-tracts, and community organizations. Formal local bodies and administrative offices do not have identical roles: elected Hluttaws represent constituencies, while bureaucratic chains coordinate personnel, budgets, and implementation (Republic of the Union of Myanmar, 2008; The Asia Foundation, 2019).

Real power depends on appointments, fiscal discretion, security, access to records, party organization, customary authority, and the ability to deliver services. These conditions vary across Myanmar, particularly after 2021. The existing evidence does not support a nationwide map of local political control.

A defensible account therefore treats local politics as locality- and period-specific interaction among formal institutions, administrative officials, security actors, and community authorities.
