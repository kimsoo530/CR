## 9.2 Prices, Exchange Rate, and Macroeconomic Stabilization

Price instability affects Myanmar through purchasing power, wage setting, import costs, debt service, and public procurement. The archived data provide separate measures of consumer-price or GDP-deflator inflation, the official exchange rate, and reserve coverage, but their years do not align. The 2019 consumer-price observation and 2024 GDP-deflator observation should not be presented as one contemporaneous inflation estimate (World Bank, 2025).

Exchange-rate conditions transmit external pressure into domestic administration. Imported fuel, medicine, machinery, and food become more expensive when the currency depreciates, while remittances and export receipts may rise in local-currency terms without equivalent real gains.

The evidence supports a stability mechanism, not a complete current diagnosis. Reliable interpretation requires consistent dates and a clear distinction among official, market, and converted values.
