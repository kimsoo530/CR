## 12.1 Labor Force, Participation, and Informality

Myanmar's labor market affects household income, migration, tax capacity, and the supply of skills to public and private organizations. ILOSTAT provides comparable labor indicators, but participation, employment, unemployment, and informality are not interchangeable measures and retain different reference definitions (International Labour Organization, 2025).

A low unemployment rate would not by itself indicate a healthy labor market if people are underemployed, outside the labor force, working informally, or migrating. Agriculture and small-scale services can absorb labor while offering limited security or productivity. Conflict and displacement can further change where people work and whether surveys observe them.

The evidence supports a labor-market framework rather than a single employment diagnosis. Current locality-specific labor disruption and informal employment measurement remain incomplete.
