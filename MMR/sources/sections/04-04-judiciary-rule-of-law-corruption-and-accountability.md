## 4.4 Judiciary, Rule of Law, Corruption, and Accountability

Myanmar's constitutional judiciary includes ordinary courts, Courts-Martial, and the Constitutional Tribunal. The formal design assigns courts jurisdiction and review functions, but legal structure alone does not establish judicial independence, access, or enforcement (Republic of the Union of Myanmar, 2008).

Accountability depends on appointments, budgets, prosecutorial practice, administrative records, anti-corruption institutions, and the ability of citizens to obtain remedies. The World Bank governance indicators are useful comparative signals, but they do not identify which institution caused a particular outcome or whether protection is uniform across the country (World Bank, 2025).

The current evidence supports a formal-versus-effective rule-of-law distinction. Post-2021 judicial operation and locality-level access remain incompletely verified.
