## 7.5 Fiscal Capacity and Public Sector Risks

### 7.5 Post-2021 Fiscal Disruption and Conflict Political Economy

The post-2021 fiscal question has at least three layers. First, the formal fiscal architecture remains the constitutional and legal design described above. Second, SAC-associated documents and institutions may publish official fiscal claims or operate formal budget processes. Third, international series and independent diagnostics may record outcomes or estimates that do not necessarily match official claims. These layers cannot be collapsed into a single account of fiscal performance.

The available IMF series show broad fiscal indicators through 2024, but they do not by themselves identify how conflict, sanctions, foreign-exchange restrictions or institutional fragmentation produced the observed values. Nor do the archived data establish which authority collected, controlled or spent resources in contested areas. The statistical series are therefore descriptive signals, while causal attribution remains unresolved.

Conflict-related political economy is an additional, actor-specific field. Parallel taxation, resource extraction, border-economy finance and armed-actor revenue systems may be important, but no verified local quantitative package was found for this batch. One locality or one actor's reported collection cannot be generalized to Myanmar as a whole. Any later claim in this field requires identification of the actor, locality, date, instrument, revenue base and degree of independent corroboration.

The correct analytical separation is consequently: formal public finance describes constitutional and official budget institutions; international statistics describe comparable fiscal indicators; conflict political economy describes actor-specific and geographically bounded revenue arrangements. The three may interact, but the current evidence does not justify combining them into one national fiscal total.

### 7.6 Fiscal Capacity and Governance Implications

Fiscal capacity is the ability to mobilize, authorize, allocate, spend, monitor and correct public resources. The Constitution documents the authority and intergovernmental channels through which these tasks are supposed to occur. The statistical appendix shows a broad fiscal envelope with fiscal-balance and debt pressure in the archived IMF series, alongside a narrower central-government evidence layer ending in 2019 in the World Bank series. The IMF expenditure series remains unresolved.

The central governance implication is evidentiary as much as fiscal. Myanmar's formal architecture can be described with reasonable confidence, but current performance cannot be inferred from the existence of the Financial Commission, Union Fund, Region/State funds or Auditor-General. Similarly, international estimates cannot replace national accounts, budget execution or audit follow-up. The post-2021 fiscal system is therefore a formally structured but empirically incomplete field in which public-finance performance, official claims and conflict-related finance remain distinct.

The strongest defensible conclusion is qualified: Myanmar has a documented constitutional fiscal architecture and a reproducible set of international fiscal indicators, but its current public-finance operation, SOE exposure, subnational transfers, audit performance and conflict-related revenue fragmentation remain insufficiently verified for a national fiscal diagnosis.
