## 4.1 Historical Formation of the Political Order

Myanmar's political order combines constitutional design, military institutionalization, electoral episodes, and conflict over authority. The formal sequence matters because the 2008 Constitution embeds the Defence Services in representative and security institutions, while the post-2021 period produced competing claims about emergency authority and constitutional continuity (Republic of the Union of Myanmar, 2008; United Nations Human Rights Council, 2021).

Political history should therefore be read through institutions and operating conditions. Elections, parties, legislatures, courts, and executives are not separate from the military and territorial questions analysed in Chapters 3 and 15. Their practical significance depends on whether rules are accepted, implemented, and available across localities.

The available archive supports this institutional interpretation. It does not justify treating a single political indicator as a complete measure of Myanmar's democratic trajectory.
