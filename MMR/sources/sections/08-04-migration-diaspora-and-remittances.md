## 8.4 Migration, Diaspora, and Remittances

Migration links Myanmar's labor market, household welfare, border relations, and foreign-exchange position. The archived World Bank series records personal remittances received at US$2.55 billion in 2019, but the series is in current US dollars and does not show how receipts are distributed across households, regions, or transfer channels (World Bank, 2025). Its latest available year also predates the report's post-2021 conflict and displacement evidence.

Remittances can stabilize consumption and finance education, health, housing, or migration costs. They can also signal limited domestic employment opportunities and dependence on destination-country labor markets. The policy mechanism is therefore two-sided: transfer income may cushion households, while migration can remove working-age people and weaken local labor supply.

The current archive does not establish informal transfers, exchange-rate effects, or the distribution of migrant workers by destination. These remain research gaps.
