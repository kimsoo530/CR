### 7.4 Public Enterprises, Resources, and Public-Sector Finance

Myanmar's public-sector fiscal analysis requires evidence on state-owned enterprises, energy and mining, public borrowing, transfers, dividends, arrears and contingent liabilities. The current local archive does not contain verified SOE financial statements, a consolidated public-sector balance sheet, a resource-revenue register or a current contingent-liability report. The candidate therefore does not estimate SOE revenue, resource rents or military-business income.

This is a substantive gap rather than a minor data omission. SOEs can affect fiscal exposure through transfers to the budget, retained earnings, borrowing, arrears, subsidized prices, guarantees and access to foreign exchange. Resource industries can affect public finance through royalties, taxes, production-sharing arrangements and off-budget or semi-public channels. Without verified accounts and institutional definitions, however, it is not possible to determine the scale or direction of these effects for Myanmar in this candidate.

The same caution applies to military expenditure. International indicators may provide a comparable signal, but they are not equivalent to a Myanmar national budget line and cannot establish military-business revenue or conflict-finance flows. Official budget data, international comparable indicators and conflict political economy remain analytically separate.
