Defense spending belongs in fiscal analysis because security organizations compete for labor, capital, procurement capacity, and budget space. The relevant question is not only whether military expenditure is high, but whether security commitments create opportunity costs for development spending and whether personnel levels indicate a large uniformed claim on the labor force.

Defense and public-security spending in Myanmar should be read as a fiscal commitment as well as a security indicator. Armed forces personnel account for 1.9% of the total labor force in the latest World Bank series. Military expenditure is 6.8% of GDP and 29.1% of government expenditure. A country can have modest military expenditure as a share of GDP but still face high opportunity costs if the overall budget is small, revenue is weak, or internal-security obligations compete with education, health, maintenance, and local services.
