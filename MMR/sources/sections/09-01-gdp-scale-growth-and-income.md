Macroeconomic analysis of Myanmar begins with a disciplined framework. GDP scale shows the size of the economy and the potential tax base; real GDP growth shows momentum and cyclical pressure; GDP per capita places the development challenge at the level of household income. These indicators should be read together before moving to prices, trade, or sectoral structure.

| Diagnostic question | Core indicators | Interpretive use |

| How large is the economy and what income level does it support? | GDP, GDP growth, GDP per capita | Separates economic scale, short-run momentum, and household income constraints. |

| Are nominal conditions stable? | CPI inflation, GDP deflator inflation, exchange rate, reserves | Shows whether households, firms, and public budgets operate under predictable prices and external buffers. |

| Does the economy earn enough foreign exchange? | Exports, imports, trade balance, current account, openness | Connects trade performance to external vulnerability and import-financing needs. |

| What produces growth? | Agriculture, industry, services shares | Distinguishes diversified service-led growth from commodity, construction, or narrow industrial dependence. |
