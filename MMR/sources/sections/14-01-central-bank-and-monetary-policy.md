## 14.1 Central Bank and Monetary Policy

Monetary policy affects prices, exchange rates, bank liquidity, public financing, and household purchasing power. The Central Bank's formal role must be distinguished from observed monetary transmission: legal authority does not establish policy independence, market confidence, or uniform access to liquidity.

Myanmar's macroeconomic evidence shows why reference periods and definitions matter. Inflation, exchange-rate, reserve, and fiscal indicators come from different series and cannot be combined without preserving units and source coverage (International Monetary Fund, 2025; World Bank, 2025). Post-2021 conditions are also affected by conflict, foreign-exchange constraints, and institutional uncertainty, but causal attribution requires direct evidence.

The archive supports an institutional monetary framework, not a complete current assessment of policy effectiveness.
