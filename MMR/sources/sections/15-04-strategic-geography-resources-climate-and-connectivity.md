## 15.4 Strategic Geography, Resources, Climate, and Connectivity

### 15.6 Geopolitical Assessment

Myanmar's geopolitical position is consequential because its external relations are transmitted through institutional fragility, border connectivity and conflict. The formal constitutional design centralizes foreign affairs and security authority at the Union level. The post-2021 evidence shows a more complicated external environment in which diplomatic representation was procedurally contested, ASEAN relied on a defined but implementation-constrained framework, and humanitarian actors operated amid conflict and restricted access.

The strongest conclusion is therefore conditional. Myanmar is not adequately described either as an ordinary ASEAN foreign-policy case or as a simple proxy arena for great-power competition. Its external position is shaped by the interaction of Union-level institutions, competing actor claims, UN representation procedures, ASEAN diplomacy, neighbouring-state channels and humanitarian operations. The available evidence supports this institutional and mechanism-based interpretation. It does not support claims about settled recognition, uniform foreign alignment, nationwide territorial control, or precise external financing of armed actors.

This analysis connects to Chapter 3 through the constitutional foreign-affairs and NDSC provisions; to Chapter 4 through the distinction between formal institutions and post-2021 power claims; to Chapter 5 through uneven administration and access; to Chapter 10 through borders and external economic constraints; to Chapter 13 through security-related service disruption; and to Chapters 16 and 17 through diplomatic contestation and humanitarian pressure as constraints on national strategy and integrated state capacity.
