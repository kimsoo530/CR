## 1.5 Evidence Base

The report uses four evidence layers. Constitutions, laws, official orders, and government documents establish formal rules or the positions of issuing institutions. World Bank, IMF, ILO, and WHO datasets provide comparable indicators with source-specific years and definitions. Myanmar governance research interprets administrative mechanisms. UN, ASEAN, and humanitarian records provide external observations of conflict, diplomacy, displacement, and service conditions.

These layers answer different questions. A constitutional article does not demonstrate implementation; an official announcement does not independently verify performance; an aggregate indicator does not identify local authority; and a humanitarian planning estimate is not a census. The high-risk chapters retain these distinctions explicitly.

The archive is strongest for formal documents and selected international series. It is weaker for current subnational finance, territorial reach, conflict-related revenue, service outcomes across localities, and post-2021 implementation. Those limitations remain part of the country diagnosis.
