# Organizational Management and Bureaucratic Reform Literature Note

This note records the comparative public administration literature used to frame section 5.2 of the country report.

- Andrews, M. (2013). *The limits of institutional reform in development: Changing rules for realistic solutions*. Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139060974
- Christensen, T., & Laegreid, P. (2007). The whole-of-government approach to public sector reform. *Public Administration Review, 67*(6), 1059-1066. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6210.2007.00797.x
- Evans, P., & Rauch, J. E. (1999). Bureaucracy and growth: A cross-national analysis of the effects of Weberian state structures on economic growth. *American Sociological Review, 64*(5), 748-765. https://doi.org/10.1177/000312249906400508
- Grindle, M. S. (1997). Divergent cultures? When public organizations perform well in developing countries. *World Development, 25*(4), 481-495. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0305-750X(96)00123-4
- Pollitt, C., & Bouckaert, G. (2017). *Public management reform: A comparative analysis - Into the age of austerity* (4th ed.). Oxford University Press.

Country-specific editions should add national civil-service laws, cabinet or government-organization laws, public administration reform strategies, ministry restructuring decrees, digital-government strategies, OECD or World Bank public governance reviews, and peer-reviewed country studies.
