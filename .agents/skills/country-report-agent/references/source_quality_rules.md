# Source Quality Rules

Use this reference when auditing sources, citations, numerical claims, and evidence gaps. For APA in-text citation matching, final References, and source-existence verification, also read `citation_reference_integrity.md`. For register field standards, read `reference_schemas.md`. For audit tables, read `output_templates.md`.

## Source Roles

- Official legal and government documents explain institutional design.
- National official statistics support latest-year and granular country evidence.
- International databases support comparability and long-run trends.
- International diagnostic reports explain reform constraints and policy sequencing.
- BTI country reports and indicators support interpretation of democracy, market-economy transformation, governance performance, and reform steering capacity for developing and transition countries.
- V-Dem datasets and Democracy Reports support comparative analysis of democratic institutions, autocratization, civil liberties, electoral democracy, and political-regime change.
- QoG datasets support comparative analysis of corruption, quality of government, administrative capacity, public goods, and related social indicators.
- Academic sources identify contested interpretations, theory links, and political-economy debates.
- News can signal recent salience but should not be the main source for structural claims.

## Comparative Governance Sources

Use these sources as comparative and diagnostic evidence, not as substitutes for country-specific official documents:

| Source | Best use | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Bertelsmann Stiftung BTI Country Reports and Transformation Index | Country narrative plus scores on democracy, market economy, governance, transformation, and reform capacity | Focuses on political/economic transformation and governance; does not cover the full administrative, fiscal, and social scope of this report |
| V-Dem Institute, University of Gothenburg | Large-scale democracy indicators, democratic institutions, regime change, autocratization, and annual Democracy Reports | Specialized in democracy and political institutions; many indicators are expert-coded and should be treated as diagnostic signals |
| Quality of Government Institute, University of Gothenburg | QoG datasets on corruption, government quality, public administration, institutions, services, and social indicators | Primarily a dataset infrastructure; usually supports comparative analysis rather than narrative country-report sections by itself |

When using these sources, name the dataset/report, year, indicator or score where available, and methodological limits. Do not use an index score as direct proof of administrative reality without interpretation and corroboration.

## Minimum Publication-Candidate Source Package

For high-priority reports, seek country-specific official sources for:

- Constitution, basic law, or current statehood framework
- Government organization, cabinet, or public administration structure
- Civil service or public employment framework
- Latest budget, fiscal strategy, public debt, or medium-term expenditure documents
- Audit, anti-corruption, procurement, or accountability reports
- Local government and territorial administration where relevant
- Current national development strategy or state program
- Central bank, financial-sector regulator, or monetary-policy documents
- National statistical office, census, or official data portal

Fragile, conflict-affected, partially recognized, or de facto divided countries also need:

- territorial-control or de facto authority sources
- humanitarian service-delivery diagnostics
- recognition, peace, ceasefire, or political-settlement documents where relevant

## Quantitative Claim Requirements

Every important quantitative claim should provide:

- value
- year or period
- unit
- country coverage
- source institution
- indicator, table, or dataset ID where available
- whether the value is official, estimate, projection, or project-calculated

## Red Flags

- Numeric claims without years or units
- International indicator used as if it were national official data
- Official plan treated as implemented outcome
- One source used to support a contested political claim
- BTI, V-Dem, or QoG score used without explaining indicator meaning, year, scale, and limits
- News item cited as a structural data source
- Chart footnote missing unit or indicator ID
- Mixed-unit chart without panels or clear separation
- References chapter inconsistent with in-text citations
- APA in-text citation without a matching final reference entry
- Reference entry that cannot be verified through DOI, URL, official source page, local archive, or access note
- Suspicious article, book, or report metadata that may have been invented
