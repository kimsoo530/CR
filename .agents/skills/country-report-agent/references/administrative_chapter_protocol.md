# Administrative Chapter Protocol

Use this protocol for centre-of-government, organizational reform, civil-service, financial-administration, local-delivery, digital-government, and implementation-capacity sections.

## Define The Administrative Result

State the decision, programme, transaction, staffing action, service, or correction that the section must explain. Do not substitute an institution list or reform chronology for an administrative result.

## Map Distinct Coordination Circuits

Separate political authorization, cabinet coordination, planning, budgeting, cash and accounting, personnel management, line-agency execution, performance monitoring, audit, and intergovernmental implementation. For each circuit identify:

- responsible institution and scope of authority;
- authoritative decision or administrative record;
- resource or information dependency;
- handoff to the next institution;
- observable delay, variance, or correction.

Treat the system as overlapping circuits when authority is not hierarchical. A central announcement cannot command a constitutionally or operationally distinct subnational actor without an authority, financing, coordination, and reporting mechanism.

## Distinguish Design, Activity, Output, And Outcome

A mandate establishes formal responsibility. A reform announcement establishes intent. A meeting, circular, performance bond, dashboard, or status colour establishes activity or reporting. None alone proves funded execution, completed output, service quality, or citizen outcome. Trace the same deliverable through authorization, plan, budget, release, procurement, payment, physical output, use, and correction wherever the evidence permits.

For machinery-of-government reform, classify each episode by status: proposal, executive or legislative authorization, legal effect, transfer of mandate and resources, operational stabilization, and verified outcome. Do not describe an announced merger, abolition, relocation, deadline, committee, or White Paper as completed reform. Check enabling laws, appropriations, authorized posts and actual staff dispositions, assets and liabilities, contracts, records and IT migration, audit responsibility, service continuity, transition costs, and realized rather than projected savings. A reform timeline is useful only when it distinguishes these statuses instead of presenting chronology as progress.

## Evaluate Control Against Operational Autonomy

Do not assume that more indicators, approvals, reporting, or central supervision improves performance. Use academic and administrative evidence to test whether central control resolves a coordination problem or instead increases compliance work, delays decisions, distorts reporting, or suppresses useful managerial autonomy. State the study period, unit of analysis, and identification limit before transferring older or sector-specific findings to a current reform.

## Use Administrative Statistics Precisely

Distinguish appropriation, release, commitment, expenditure, physical completion, and outcome. A forecast error is not an execution rate; a budget allocation is not cash availability; a payroll record is not a headcount audit; a digital transaction is not successful service delivery. Report year, unit, denominator, administrative coverage, and source record.

For civil-service workforce claims, define the employment universe before reporting a number. Keep the core civil service, wider public service, payroll-platform enrolment, general-government employment, state and local workforces, public enterprises, and household-survey employment estimates separate. If no harmonized official total is available, state the gap; do not manufacture one by adding or subtracting incompatible sources. Reconcile apparent conflicts by checking reference date, institutional coverage, active-versus-paid status, duplicates and exclusions.

Treat grade level, step, cadre, authorized post, rank or title, and salary structure as separate variables. Do not invent a universal title-to-grade mapping across occupational schemes, and do not describe an apex appointed office as an additional grade unless an authoritative rule does so. A useful personnel audit links the authorized post to the person, cadre, grade, verified competence, duty station, payroll record, performance commitment and service output while preserving the different legal purposes of each system.

For financial administration, do not use the deficit, debt stock, appropriation, release, commitment, payment, physical completion, audit finding, recovery, or prosecution as substitutes for one another. Trace a stable programme or contract identifier through forecast, legal authorization, cash plan, commitment, tender and award, invoice and acceptance, payment, accounting, physical result, audit, management response, legislative disposition, and closure. Record the institution and timestamp at every handoff.

Budget transparency and PFM scores are disclosure or system diagnostics, not proof of spending quality. Where reports are eventually published, measure period-end-to-publication lag and whether the classification remains comparable with the enacted budget. A procurement portal establishes capability only; calculate disclosure or competition rates only when the denominator covers the same procurement population. An audit report establishes answerability, not correction: verify the follow-up policy, management response, legislative recommendation, recovery or sanction, repeat finding, and closure date.

## Design Useful Visual Evidence

Use a table, flow diagram, Sankey-style handoff map, or timeline only when it clarifies responsibility, resources, delay, or correction. Keep exact institution names and direction of authority. Prefer editable HTML, SVG, chart code, or Markdown tables for precise administrative relationships. Use generated imagery for conceptual explanation only, never as the sole source of institutional facts or quantitative values. Do not create the same process diagram for every section.

## Derive Observable Reform Implications

Tie each recommendation to a diagnosed handoff or bottleneck. Name the responsible institution, required record, correction authority, implementation sequence, and success measure. Prefer common programme identifiers, timestamps, variance codes, release and procurement links, completion evidence, and documented corrective action over generic calls for stronger coordination.

## Chapter Boundary Checks

- Centre-of-government: coordination circuits and cross-government traceability.
- Organizational reform: mandate overlap, restructuring, sequencing, and institutional memory.
- Civil service: recruitment, deployment, capability, incentives, careers, and performance management.
- Financial administration: authorization, cash, procurement, accounting, audit, and correction.
- Local delivery: assignment, subnational authority, finance, frontline capability, and citizen accountability.
- Digital administration: identity, interoperability, transaction integrity, privacy, appeal, and exclusion.
- Implementation synthesis: interactions and bottlenecks demonstrated by prior sections, not a new composite score without defensible construction.

End each section by advancing the next role rather than repeating a generic reform checklist.
