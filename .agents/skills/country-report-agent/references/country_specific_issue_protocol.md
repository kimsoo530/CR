# Country-Specific Issue Protocol

Use this reference when deciding whether a country report needs sections beyond the standard chapter structure, where those sections should be placed, and how to keep them analytically grounded.

## Core Principle

Maintain the standard country-report chapters for comparability, but add or preserve country-specific issue sections when they are essential to understanding the country's institutions, state capacity, political economy, development path, social structure, territorial condition, or external position.

Do not create special sections merely because a topic is interesting. Add them only when the issue changes how readers should interpret the country.

## Standard Plus Country-Specific Model

Use two layers:

1. **Standard chapters**: Keep the common 1-19 chapter structure so reports remain comparable across countries.
2. **Country-specific issue sections**: Add focused sections inside the most relevant standard chapter when a national issue cannot be handled adequately by generic coverage.

Avoid creating many new top-level chapters. Prefer adding or renaming sections within existing chapters.

## Selection Criteria

Treat an issue as country-specific when at least two of these conditions are met:

- It is central to the country's institutional operation or state capacity.
- It explains a major divergence between formal rules and actual practice.
- It shapes fiscal, administrative, social, security, or development constraints.
- It is repeatedly emphasized by official plans, diagnostic reports, academic literature, or reliable data.
- It distinguishes the country from regional or income-group peers.
- It affects multiple chapters and must be integrated in chapter 17.
- It is essential for interpreting recent reform, conflict, statehood, regime, demographic, or economic trajectory.

Reject or defer an issue when:

- it is only a recent news event without structural significance
- it duplicates an existing section
- it lacks verifiable sources
- it is too broad to answer in one section
- it would make the report less comparable without adding real country insight

## Placement Rules

Place the issue where it explains the main institutional or policy mechanism:

| Issue type | Preferred chapter |
|---|---|
| statehood, constitutional ambiguity, recognition, sovereignty | 3 or 15 |
| regime structure, ruling coalition, elite politics, party system | 4 |
| administrative reform, civil service, decentralization, territorial administration | 5 |
| corruption, accountability, rule of law, implementation capacity | 6 |
| fiscal dependence, debt, public finance, SOE fiscal risk, resource revenue | 7 |
| demography, migration, refugees, ethnic or regional structure | 8 |
| macro vulnerability, inflation, growth model, resource dependence | 9 |
| trade corridors, sanctions, FDI, remittances, external dependence | 10 |
| water, energy, transport, digital, climate infrastructure | 11 |
| labor migration, informality, skills, youth employment | 12 |
| education, health, welfare, gender, human development | 13 |
| banking, central bank, financial inclusion, dollarization | 14 |
| conflict, security, geopolitics, alliances, border disputes | 15 |
| national development strategy, reform sequencing, implementation agenda | 16 |
| cross-cutting operating model, future risk, comparative lessons | 17 |

If an issue affects multiple chapters, write the substantive section in the chapter with the strongest mechanism and return to the issue synthetically in chapter 17.

## Common Country-Specific Issue Families

Use these as prompts for diagnosis, not as mandatory topics:

- statehood, recognition, territorial control, de facto authority
- post-conflict reconstruction, peace settlement, humanitarian service delivery
- dominant-party rule, monarchy, military influence, theocracy, transitional authority
- center-local relations, federalism, decentralization, disputed regions
- resource dependence, sovereign wealth, extractive governance, commodity volatility
- sanctions, aid dependence, remittances, debt distress, balance-of-payments constraint
- migration, refugee hosting, diaspora, labor export, demographic aging, youth bulge
- water scarcity, food security, energy transition, climate vulnerability
- strategic corridors, ports, landlockedness, logistics, regional integration
- state-owned enterprises, industrial policy, privatization, public investment model
- digital state, e-government, surveillance capacity, data governance
- gender, education access, health system capacity, social protection model
- corruption, procurement, audit institutions, informal governance

## Issue Discovery Workflow

1. Read the overview, chapter titles, quality audit, source gap plan, and section evidence plan.
2. List issues that appear repeatedly across sources or chapters.
3. For each issue, identify why it is structurally important.
4. Choose the best standard chapter placement.
5. Decide whether the issue needs a new section, a renamed section, or only a paragraph inside an existing section.
6. Record the issue in the evidence plan with priority, source needs, and chapter links.
7. Ensure chapter 17 synthesizes the most important cross-cutting issues.

## Section Design

A country-specific issue section should have:

- a precise section title
- a country-specific central claim
- official or diagnostic sources that show why the issue matters
- statistical evidence when the issue has a measurable dimension
- historical or institutional context when needed
- a clear connection to state capacity, implementation, policy risk, or comparative interpretation
- a short explanation of how the issue affects other chapters

Avoid:

- generic titles such as `Special Issues`
- dumping unrelated topics into one section
- building a section around a single news event
- treating an official plan as an achieved outcome
- adding a country-specific section without explaining why it belongs in that chapter

## Section Naming

Prefer descriptive titles that name the mechanism:

- `Water Scarcity and Upstream-Downstream Coordination`
- `De Facto Authority and Service Delivery`
- `Resource Revenue and Fiscal Volatility`
- `Labor Migration and Remittance Dependence`
- `Capital-City Concentration and Regional Implementation`

Avoid vague titles:

- `Current Issues`
- `Other Topics`
- `Recent Developments`
- `Challenges`

## Evidence Requirements

Each country-specific issue should normally have at least two of these evidence types:

- official law, strategy, budget, administrative rule, or government report
- national official statistics or administrative data
- international comparable indicator or diagnostic dataset
- international organization diagnostic report
- academic article, book, or working paper
- reputable map, geospatial, conflict, trade, or sector dataset when relevant

For politically sensitive issues, use multiple source types and state limits clearly.

## Integration With Chapter 17

Chapter 17 should not simply summarize all chapters. It should identify the country's operating model.

When a country-specific issue is central, chapter 17 should explain:

- how the issue interacts with formal institutions
- what it implies for implementation capacity
- whether it is a temporary constraint or structural feature
- how it changes comparison with peer countries
- what evidence remains uncertain

## Issue Plan Output

When asked to propose country-specific sections, return a compact table:

| Issue | Why country-specific | Evidence base | Recommended placement | Section action | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water scarcity | Shapes agriculture, energy, and regional diplomacy | Official strategy, FAO, World Bank diagnostic | Chapter 11, linked to 15 and 17 | Add focused section | High |

## Quality Check

Before finalizing the table of contents, confirm:

- all standard chapters remain present
- special sections are placed inside the most relevant standard chapters
- each special section has a central claim and source plan
- no special section duplicates another section
- the number of special sections is manageable
- chapter 17 integrates the most important cross-cutting issues
